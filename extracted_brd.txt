1. Executive Summary
This plan proposes a cloud-hosted teleconsultation platform that enables patients to discover providers, book appointments, join secure video consultations, exchange relevant documents, receive prescriptions or consultation summaries where legally and clinically appropriate, and manage follow-up care. Doctors and clinic administrators receive tools for scheduling, consultation workflows, patient context, documentation and operational reporting.
The platform should be designed as a modular, API-first product with strong identity controls, privacy-by-design, auditable access, resilient video sessions and clear separation between clinical records and operational analytics. The initial release should focus on a safe, usable core rather than attempting to replace a full hospital information system.

3. Scope and Product Boundaries
3.1 MVP capabilities
Patient registration, sign-in, consent capture and profile management.
Provider directory with specialty, clinic, language, availability and consultation fee.
Appointment search, booking, rescheduling, cancellation and waitlist/basic no-show handling.
Secure video consultation with waiting room, join checks and fallback instructions.
Clinician view of appointment details and patient-provided intake information.
Consultation notes and structured summary; prescription workflow only after clinical and jurisdictional validation.
Document upload and sharing with file type/size validation, malware scanning and access controls.
Notifications by email/SMS/push through approved providers; notification content must avoid unnecessary sensitive health details.
Payment integration and refund/cancellation rules if the business model requires online payments.
Clinic/admin console for providers, schedules, appointment exceptions, service configuration and basic reporting.
Role-based access, audit trails, support tooling, operational dashboards and backup/restore procedures.
3.2 Later-phase capabilities
Native iOS and Android applications, subject to product validation.
FHIR-based interoperability and integrations with selected EHR/HIS platforms.
Multi-clinic and multi-tenant administration with tenant-level policy boundaries.
Insurance, claims, employer health programmes and corporate wellness integrations where applicable.
Multilingual patient experience, accessibility enhancements and expanded regional workflows.
AI-assisted transcription, summarization or coding only after consent, clinical validation, privacy impact assessment and human review controls.
Advanced analytics, care pathways and integrations with labs, pharmacies or remote monitoring devices.
3.3 Out of scope for initial MVP
Autonomous diagnosis, triage or treatment decisions by AI.
Emergency-care replacement; the interface must direct urgent or emergency cases to appropriate local services.
Full hospital information system, inpatient management, billing ERP or laboratory information system.
Unreviewed cross-border transfer or centralization of health data.
Recording consultations by default. If recording is ever introduced, it requires explicit product, consent, legal and retention decisions.

Successful restore and disaster-recovery exercise.
Security design principles:
Zero trust: authenticate and authorize every request; do not assume a trusted internal network.
Least privilege: separate patient, clinician, clinic administrator, support, developer and platform administrator permissions.
Secure by default: private storage, no public database endpoints, no default consultation recording, and no sensitive data in notifications.
Dual control for high-risk operations such as bulk exports, privileged access elevation and key administrative changes.
Use a recognized control framework such as NIST CSF/ISO 27001 as a governance reference; certification is a separate scoped programme, not an automatic result of using the framework.
9. Privacy, Healthcare Compliance and Clinical Governance
Compliance must be mapped to the actual launch country, provider type, data-controller/processor roles, clinical workflow and integrations. For an India-first launch, assess applicable provisions of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act and rules in force at launch, relevant telemedicine practice requirements, medical record obligations, consumer protection, electronic signatures, payment requirements and contractual obligations. If serving other markets, separately assess applicable regimes such as GDPR/UK GDPR, HIPAA where the organization and activities fall within its scope, and local telehealth licensing rules.
Appoint accountable business and clinical owners for consent, records, prescribing, escalation, complaint handling and clinical safety.
Complete a Data Protection Impact Assessment / privacy impact assessment before processing real patient data at scale.
Document consent purpose, wording, timestamp, version, withdrawal handling and the effect on ongoing care or legal record retention.
Validate clinician credentials, registration, specialties and permitted practice jurisdictions before enabling consultations.
Define which consultation types are appropriate for remote care and the escalation process when remote consultation is unsuitable.
Ensure prescription creation, signing, delivery, correction and cancellation follow applicable professional and legal requirements.
Contractually assess cloud, video, SMS/email and analytics providers as subprocessors/service providers; record locations, retention, breach notification and deletion obligations.
Set retention schedules and legal holds with healthcare/legal experts; do not assume all records can be deleted on user request.
Provide accessible privacy notices, grievance/contact mechanisms and incident/breach assessment procedures.
Do not use identifiable patient data to train AI models by default. Any proposed use needs separate legal, privacy, security, clinical and consent review.
This section identifies review areas rather than certifying compliance. Obtain advice from qualified healthcare counsel and clinical governance professionals for each operating jurisdiction.

12. Availability, Backup and Disaster Recovery
Deploy production across availability zones where supported and justified by the selected cloud region.
Use managed database high availability and point-in-time recovery where supported.
Keep encrypted backups in a separate security boundary; restrict deletion and modification permissions.
Define recovery tiers for identity, scheduling, consultation access, clinical records, payments and reporting.
Create runbooks for video-provider outage, cloud-region degradation, database recovery, compromised credentials, ransomware suspicion and data breach.
Test restore procedures at least quarterly during the pilot-to-production transition and after major architecture changes.
Document degraded modes: for example, patients can view appointment instructions if video is unavailable, while clinical documentation remains protected and consistent.
